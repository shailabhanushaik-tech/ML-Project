Understand the markers

Select a marker to read a plain-language description.
Age

Age is included as a broad context marker in this educational scoring example. Age alone cannot diagnose kidney disease.

Blood pressure

Blood pressure is the force of blood against artery walls. Persistently high readings should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Specific gravity

Urine specific gravity reflects how concentrated a urine sample is. It can vary with hydration and other factors, so it needs clinical context.

Albumin

Albumin is a protein. Its presence in urine can be one reason a clinician may order follow-up testing.

Urine sugar

Sugar detected in urine can have several explanations and should be interpreted alongside appropriate blood tests and clinical advice.

Red blood cells

Red blood cells in urine may have many causes. A single result is not enough to determine a diagnosis.

Pus cells

Pus cells are white blood cells seen in urine tests and can be associated with inflammation or infection, among other causes.

Pus cell clumps

This dataset marker records whether pus-cell clumps were reported. Ask a clinician to interpret it with the full test report.

Bacteria

Bacteria reported in a urine sample may need interpretation alongside symptoms and sample collection details.

Hypertension history

This records whether a history of high blood pressure is reported. It is one contextual marker, not a diagnosis.

Diabetes history

This records whether a history of diabetes is reported. A clinician considers it alongside many other health factors.

Appetite

Appetite is a general symptom and is not specific to kidney conditions. Many unrelated factors can affect it.
